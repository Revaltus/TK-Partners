## LinkedIn

A tasting room owner drives a 22-mile round trip to 30 nonprofit board meetings and events in a year. That is 660 miles and a $92.40 deduction. Her volunteer hours? $0, even if her time is worth $150 an hour.

The IRS never lets you deduct the value of your time. It does let you deduct unreimbursed, directly connected costs: mileage at 14 cents per mile (or actual gas and oil), parking, supplies, and qualifying overnight travel. Two catches. You have to itemize on Schedule A, and the charity must be a qualified organization. With a 2025 standard deduction of $31,500 for joint filers, a $92 deduction rarely moves the needle alone.

Winery owners get extra wrinkles. Donated wine is generally deductible at your cost, not the $38 shelf price, and it has to come out of cost of goods sold so it isn't counted twice.
