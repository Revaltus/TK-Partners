## LinkedIn

A cellar fire in October, the full vintage on the floor, and an $850,000 worst-case loss. Add three months closed afterward, another $180,000, against $150,000 in reserves.

That is the hypothetical Walla Walla winery in our latest post. It shows why insurance sizing should start with what you could lose, not with a checklist of policy types. For each risk, ask one question: could we pay for this from reserves and keep operating? If yes, take a high deductible. If no, be generous with limits.

The deductible math makes it concrete. Moving a property deductible from $1,000 to $5,000 saves $900 a year but adds $4,000 of exposure per claim, a payback of about 4.4 years. The real gaps tend to hide in book value versus replacement cost, finished wine valued at cost versus selling price, and business interruption waiting periods.

We are CPAs, not insurance agents, so a licensed agent should price and place the coverage. We can size the exposure from your books, tax picture and net worth.
