## LinkedIn

Eight days of personal use can erase a second home's tax loss.

Take a Lake Chelan house rented 80 nights at $325. Use it 12 days, under the 14-day threshold, and it's a rental: full deductions plus about $14,500 of depreciation on a $400,000 building. That means roughly $3,650 of positive cash flow and a paper loss near $9,000. Use it 20 days instead and it becomes a residence. Deductions are capped at rental income, and most of the depreciation carries forward.

Winery owners face a second wrinkle. Rental losses are generally passive, and the $25,000 allowance phases out between $100,000 and $150,000 of modified adjusted gross income. Above that, the loss is suspended rather than deducted. It isn't gone, but it changes why you'd buy the place.

Our Walla Walla CPA team walks through which days count as personal use, the under-15-day rental perk, and why the cabin should stay out of your tasting room's LLC.
