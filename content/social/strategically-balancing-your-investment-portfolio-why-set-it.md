## LinkedIn

A Walla Walla winery owner with a $4.4 million net worth often has about $3.4 million of it in vineyard, equipment, and inventory. That is 77% in one agricultural business.

In our composite example, a $700,000 outside portfolio set at 55% stocks drifted to 68.6% after five strong years with no rebalancing. If stocks then fall 30%, the drifted mix loses about $210,000. A rebalanced one loses about $168,000. That $42,000 gap lands in the same year a smoky August or a hard freeze can slow the tasting room.

The fix is a short yearly routine. Send new money to the underweight bucket first, make most of the adjustment inside retirement accounts, and choose high-basis lots in taxable accounts. Time it to your harvest and income cycle so you are never selling in a panic.

Fund selection belongs with your investment adviser. The tax and multi-year planning side is where a CPA earns a seat at the table.
