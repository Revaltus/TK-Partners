## LinkedIn

A $2.4 million winery building in Walla Walla. $51,300 of first-year depreciation without a cost segregation study. Roughly $636,000 with one.

That is the teaching example in our latest post, and the gap comes from sorting the purchase into the right buckets: $440,000 in 5- and 7-year property, $160,000 in 15-year land improvements, and the remaining $1.4 million left at 39 years. With 100 percent bonus depreciation, the shorter buckets can often be deducted in year one. At an assumed 32 percent rate, that is about $187,000 in deferred cash tax.

Renting your tasting room? Depreciation follows who paid for the asset, and the deed has little to do with it. Interior build-out often qualifies as 15-year qualified improvement property, and the post says plainly when a study adds little for tenants.

Two cautions. Early deductions are deferred rather than erased, and production-space depreciation may fold into inventory cost under uniform capitalization rules. Rules change, so bring a CPA in before the money is spent.
