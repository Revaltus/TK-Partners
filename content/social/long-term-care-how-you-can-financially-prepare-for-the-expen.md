## LinkedIn

A Walla Walla couple, 62 and 60, owns a tasting room and 20 acres of estate vineyard. Net worth: about $4.5 million. Liquid cash: roughly $400,000.

Now the hypothetical turns. One spouse needs memory care for three years at $110,000 a year. That is $330,000, most of their accessible cash, while the other spouse still needs to live on something and the winery still needs working capital.

The pressure point is not total net worth. It is which dollars pay the bill. Selling a vineyard block to fund care can mean bad timing, capital gains tax and a disrupted succession plan.

Our latest post shows winery owners how to blend long-term care insurance, VA Aid and Attendance and dedicated savings. It also covers the tax side, including how your entity structure changes the premium deduction, which is work Erin Denney, CPA, does with closely held businesses. Medicare covers little of this, and most people should start pricing options in their 50s or early 60s.
