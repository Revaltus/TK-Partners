## LinkedIn

A winemaker can recite the Brix on a block of Syrah from memory, but ask when they last took two days off in a row and the answer gets vague.

The books usually remember. Picture an owner finishing a 14-hour day in the middle of crush and saying yes to a $38,000 equipment purchase at 9 percent financing. Nobody checked how the payment lines up with the cash sitting in barrel until spring, or with the September 15 estimated tax payment. That scenario is an illustration, but the pattern is real: tired owners drop the longer-horizon questions.

Our latest post treats rest as an operating control, like a monthly bank reconciliation. It covers a $5,000 decision rule for harvest with a 48-hour pause, a season-by-season map of where Walla Walla wineries slip, and the five signals in your numbers that burnout has already arrived. It also explains how Erin Denney, CPA, approaches multi-year planning with owner-operators.
