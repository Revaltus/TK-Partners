## LinkedIn

A distributor drop in Seattle is 260 to 280 miles one way from Walla Walla. Add the weekly vineyard runs, supply trips to the Tri-Cities and a few events in Woodinville, and a winery owner lands near 20,000 miles a year without feeling like they drive much.

That is why the lease payment on the dealer's sheet is the wrong number to check first. Most lease deals cap you at 10,000 to 12,000 miles, with overage fees of 15 to 30 cents a mile. Go 6,000 miles over across a three-year term and you could owe $900 to $1,800 at turn-in.

In our $58,000 truck example, the lease looks about $2,300 cheaper over three years. Add 6,000 excess miles and the gap is gone. Hold the truck six or seven years and buying pulls well ahead.

Mileage, business-use percentage and Section 179 limits decide this more than the monthly payment does. The post also covers when a smaller payment before a release can still make leasing worth a look.
