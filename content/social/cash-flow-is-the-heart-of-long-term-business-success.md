## LinkedIn

A Walla Walla winery shows $90,000 of net profit and still needs $161,000 of cash cushion to get through the year.

That is the illustrative example in our latest post, and the math will look familiar to anyone who has watched a vintage age in barrel. Inventory grew by $140,000. Wholesale receivables rose $35,000 because distributors pay 30 to 60 days out. Loan principal took $48,000, and owner estimated taxes took $28,000. None of those lines reduce profit. All of them drain the bank account.

The post shows how to map your cash loop season by season and find the four clots: slow receivables, over-aged inventory, tax bills, and debt payments. Then it sets up the checkups that catch a shortfall early: monthly reviews, a 13-week forecast, and quarterly tax projections. Erin Denney, CPA, our Tax Manager, works on entity structure and multi-year tax strategy for owner-operators, so the tax piece gets planned before the deadline instead of after.
