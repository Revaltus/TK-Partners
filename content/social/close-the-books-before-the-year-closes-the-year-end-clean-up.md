## LinkedIn

A $14,000 "Uncategorized" account. A savings account nobody has reconciled since August. A family grocery run coded to "Winemaking supplies."

That is a typical January file at a Walla Walla winery. The mistakes themselves are not the expensive part. The expensive part is the hours your preparer spends rebuilding the year before filing can even start.

Our year-end checklist for winery owners follows the order that saves the most time. Reconcile every bank, card, loan, and payment-processor account. Record personal spending as owner draws. Recode misfiled entries, like an equipment loan payment where only the interest is an expense. Then count inventory near December 31 and tie it to the books.

Small gaps add up. A tasting room processor that deposits $8,940 on $9,200 of sales understates revenue by $260 and hides the card fees.

A few weeks of December clean-up turns tax prep into a review instead of a rescue. It also gives you real profit numbers while you can still decide on equipment and retirement contributions before the year closes.
