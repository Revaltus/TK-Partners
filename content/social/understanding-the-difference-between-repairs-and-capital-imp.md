## LinkedIn

The same $20,000 invoice can be a $4,800 federal tax deduction this year or about $513 a year for 39 years. The difference is one word: repair or improvement.

For a Walla Walla winery owner in the 24% bracket, that gap is real cash. The IRS tangible property rules sort spending with three tests: betterment, adaptation, and restoration. Patch the tasting room roof for $1,800 and you deduct it. Replace the whole roof for $48,000 and it becomes an asset, because you replaced a substantial structural part of the building.

The barrel room cooling unit is where it gets interesting. A $3,200 compressor swap is usually a repair. A $38,000 larger system is usually capitalized. A $1,100 pump motor often clears the de minimis safe harbor without a debate.

Capitalizing is not always a loss, either. Section 179 and bonus depreciation can often pull the deduction back into the year you spend.
