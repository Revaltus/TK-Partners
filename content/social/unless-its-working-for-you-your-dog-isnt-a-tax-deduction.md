## LinkedIn

A Walla Walla winery owner brings in a guard dog after a rural break-in. Food, vet care and training run $2,700 in year one, which is potentially deductible as a vineyard security expense. Her retriever, who rides along to the tasting room? Zero.

Same species, same winery, different facts. Under IRS Publication 535, a cost has to be ordinary and necessary for the business, so what the dog does matters more than what you call it. A trained service animal can qualify as a medical expense under Publication 502, but only if you itemize and clear the 7.5 percent of AGI floor. At a $120,000 AGI, the first $9,000 gets you nothing.

The fix is simple: write a one-paragraph description of the dog's job when you get it, keep training records, and code vet and food bills to "Security" or "Vineyard operations," never "Miscellaneous." If you run an LLC or S corporation, the entity should own the dog and pay its bills directly. Erin Denney, CPA, our Tax Manager, covers that entity detail in the post.
