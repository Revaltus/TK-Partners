## Farm books that keep up with the seasons

Farm income shows up in lumps: a harvest check, a cattle sale, a crop insurance payment. Expenses never take a day off. Seed, fuel, feed, labor and repairs keep coming, so by midsummer most owners can't say where they stand, and the books are three months behind.

## Budgets and bookkeeping for unpredictable income

Farm budgeting is hard when revenue swings with yields, prices and weather. A budget built on last year's numbers breaks by June. So we build it as a range: what you must cover, what you expect, and what a bad year would look like.

Day to day, our Virtual Bookkeeper offering keeps your agricultural bookkeeping current every month. Transactions are categorized, accounts reconciled, and you get cash-flow views that answer real questions. Can you cover the equipment note until harvest? What does the crew cost per acre? Payroll handles seasonal labor, from the spring hire-up to the fall wind-down, so your crew is paid correctly and filings go in on time. It is farm record keeping that holds up when a lender or tax preparer asks for it.

Then there's the part owners notice first. Bookkeeping runs on a fixed monthly fee, with no billing surprises. No hourly invoice landing in the middle of harvest, no wondering what a phone call cost. You know the number before the month starts.

## Planning equipment and land purchases before the tax bill lands

Most tax surprises on a farm start with a purchase. A new combine, a tractor, a land parcel, an expansion. The check clears in spring, the tax bill shows up months later, and nobody ran the numbers in between.

Equipment and buildings lose value on paper over time, and how that depreciation is scheduled changes what you owe each year. Land and expansion decisions carry their own cash-flow and tax effects. Year-round advisory support means that conversation happens before you sign, not after the dealer has your deposit. Good agricultural tax planning looks at the purchase, your income so far this year, and what the next two or three seasons might bring.

That longer view is where Erin Denney, CPA, comes in. She is our Tax Manager, and her focus is tax planning for closely held businesses: entity structuring and multi-year strategy for owner-operators. For a family-held farm, that can mean asking whether your current structure still fits and how to time big moves across several years instead of cramming them into one.

## Keeping grants and subsidies clean and documented

Grants and subsidies are income with strings attached. Some are taxable, some are restricted to certain spending, and nearly all need paperwork you can produce later. Mixed in with regular operating deposits, they cause confusion fast. Here is how we keep them straight:

- Grant and subsidy funds recorded separately from sales, so agricultural income tax reporting is accurate
- Matching expenses tracked against each program
- Supporting documents filed with the transaction they belong to
- Questions answered before the return is prepared, not in the middle of it

Records move between farm and firm through our secure, paperless process. You upload documents digitally and e-sign your return, from the shop or the truck cab if that's where you are. Farm tax preparation shouldn't require a drive into town during planting.
