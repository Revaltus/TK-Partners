## Start with the structure your operation will grow into

A winery adds a tasting room and starts shipping through distributors. A rancher signs for another parcel and a piece of equipment that needs its own payment plan. A restaurateur opens a second location across town. Each owner is making a big move, and each move asks the same quiet question: is the business built to hold what comes next?

Business foundation services in Walla Walla at TK Partners answer it with two things: an entity type analysis and an accounting system setup. Our CPAs in the Walla Walla and Moses Lake offices sit down with you before anything is signed, explain the choices in plain language, and build a structure that fits how you earn money. You get a coach in your corner, not a form-filler. Think of it as pouring the concrete before you frame the house.

## Choosing between an LLC, S corporation and C corporation

The right answer depends on your operation, which is why our entity type analysis starts with your numbers. We weigh four things: your tax exposure, how you will pay yourself as an owner, your personal liability, and what happens at a future sale or succession. Whether you are weighing an S-corp vs LLC question or looking at LLC formation in Walla Walla, the goal is the same: a business entity selection you will still be glad of in five years.

- **Winery:** Several owners, an alcohol license and inventory held in barrels and bottles across vintages. How profits are split and how the license is held both shape which entity fits.
- **Agriculture:** Family ownership, land and equipment depreciation. Structure decides how those assets are held and how they pass to the next generation.
- **Restaurant:** Owner pay, payroll and tip complexity. The entity you choose changes how you draw income and how payroll is set up.

A wrong choice is expensive to unwind, and it usually surfaces at the worst time, like a sale or a tax bill. Erin Denney, CPA, our Tax Manager, builds this work around entity structuring and multi-year tax strategy for owner-operators, so today's decision still makes sense in year five.

We advise on structure and tax impact. State filing and legal drafting steps are coordinated with your attorney.
