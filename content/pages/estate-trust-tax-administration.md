## Fiduciary tax work we take off your plate

From trust tax return preparation in Walla Walla to beneficiary tax reporting, here is what lands on our desk instead of yours. Thresholds and deadlines change, so we confirm the current federal and Washington rules before anything is filed.

### Fiduciary income tax returns
icon: FileText

Form 1041 is the income tax return for an estate or a trust. It reports what the estate or trust earned and what it passed along to beneficiaries.

### Beneficiary reporting and K-1s
icon: Users

A K-1 tells each beneficiary what share of the income to report on their own return. We prepare them so nobody gets a surprise in April.

### The final return for the person who died
icon: FileCheck

The decedent's last individual income tax return still has to be filed. We handle it and make sure it lines up with the estate's return.

### Federal and Washington estate tax filings
icon: Scale

When an estate is large enough, federal and Washington each have their own estate tax filings. We check whether yours triggers one and prepare it if so.

### Basis tracking
icon: Calculator

Basis is the starting value used to figure gain when an asset is later sold. Inherited assets often get a new basis, and we document it so heirs aren't guessing years from now.

### Gift tax questions
icon: Wallet

Some gifts have to be reported even when no tax is due. We help you sort out which ones, before or after a transition.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Estate and trust tax administration

**Q: What does estate and trust tax administration include?**
A: It covers the tax work a trustee or executor takes on: the final individual return for the person who died, fiduciary income tax returns on Form 1041, beneficiary K-1s, and any federal or Washington estate tax filings. It also includes basis tracking, so heirs know their starting value when they sell an inherited asset.

**Q: Do I need a CPA to serve as executor or trustee?**
A: A CPA isn't required to serve, but the tax side is where first-time fiduciaries most often get stuck. A CPA keeps returns accurate and on time, tracks basis, and helps you document decisions so you're protected if a beneficiary or the IRS asks questions later. An attorney handles the legal side.

**Q: What documents should I bring to the first meeting?**
A: Bring the will or trust document, your letter of appointment if you have one, a copy of the death certificate, recent account and brokerage statements, property records, and recent tax returns for the person who died. Missing something? Bring what you have, and we'll help you track down the rest.

**Q: Does an estate or trust have to file its own tax return?**
A: Frequently, yes. An estate or trust that earns income after the date of death may owe its own return on Form 1041, and the rules depend on the income and the type of trust. We review the facts, confirm current thresholds, and tell you plainly whether a filing is required.

**Q: Do you work with winery and farm families on estates?**
A: Yes. Winery and agricultural estates raise questions about valuing vineyards, land, equipment and inventory, and about keeping the operation running during settlement. TK Partners specializes in wineries and agriculture, so we understand depreciation, unpredictable income, and the basis questions that come with inherited property.
